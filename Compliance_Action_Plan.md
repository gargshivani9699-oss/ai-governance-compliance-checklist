# AI Governance Compliance Action Plan

## 1. Purpose

This action plan defines corrective actions to address potential AI governance and compliance gaps identified in the Compliance Gap Assessment.

It establishes remediation priorities, ownership, target timelines, expected evidence and closure criteria.

The assessment is based on a hypothetical AI recruitment system and uses ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF 1.0 as reference frameworks.

## 2. Remediation Principles

- Prioritize actions according to risk severity.
- Assign a clearly accountable action owner.
- Establish realistic target completion dates.
- Maintain evidence of implementation.
- Validate operating effectiveness before closure.
- Escalate overdue or unresolved high-risk actions.
- Reassess residual risk after corrective actions.

## 3. Priority Classification

| Priority | Meaning | Target Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| P1 - Critical | Immediate attention required | Within 7 days |
| P2 - High | Urgent remediation required | Within 30 days |
| P3 - Medium | Planned remediation | Within 60 days |
| P4 - Low | Routine improvement | Within 90 days |

*These are illustrative internal planning targets, not mandatory ISO or NIST deadlines.*

## 5. Implementation Workflow

### Phase 1: Immediate Risk Reduction
- Review critical AI risks.
- Validate fairness and impact assessment evidence.
- Escalate unresolved critical risks to management.
- Consider restricting or pausing high-risk system use where necessary to prevent harm.

### Phase 2: Control Strengthening
- Formalize governance responsibilities.
- Improve data governance and privacy safeguards.
- Strengthen human oversight and access controls.
- Establish performance monitoring.

### Phase 3: Governance Maturity
- Maintain an updated AI inventory.
- Establish incident management procedures.
- Conduct periodic internal reviews.
- Integrate lessons learned into governance processes.

## 6. Monitoring and Reporting

The AI Governance Lead should periodically monitor:

- Open and overdue corrective actions.
- Critical and high-priority unresolved gaps.
- Evidence submitted by action owners.
- Control testing results.
- Repeat findings.
- Residual risk after remediation.

Progress should be reported to the appropriate management or governance committee.

## 7. Closure Criteria

A corrective action may be closed only when:

1. The action has been implemented.
2. Supporting documentation and evidence are available.
3. The relevant control has been tested.
4. The identified weakness has been adequately addressed.
5. Residual risk has been reassessed.
6. Closure has been reviewed and documented.

If evidence is insufficient, the action must remain open or be marked as pending verification.

## 8. Escalation

- Overdue critical actions should be escalated promptly.
- High-risk unresolved findings should be reported to senior management.
- Management risk acceptance must be documented with justification and an accountable approver.
- Risk acceptance does not replace the auditor's independent assessment.

## 9. Expected Outcomes

- Defined remediation ownership.
- Risk-based corrective action tracking.
- Improved control effectiveness.
- Evidence-based closure decisions.
- Better accountability and governance oversight.
- A repeatable compliance improvement process.

## 10. Disclaimer

This is an educational portfolio action plan for a hypothetical AI recruitment system. Owners, timelines and action statuses are illustrative and do not represent actual organizational remediation activities or formal certification findings.
