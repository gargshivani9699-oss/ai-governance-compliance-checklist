# AI Governance Compliance Gap Assessment

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured assessment of potential gaps in an organization's AI governance arrangements.

The assessment uses a hypothetical AI recruitment system and draws on ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0).

The objective is to identify control weaknesses, assess their potential impact, prioritize remediation and support continuous improvement.

## 2. Assessment Approach

Each identified gap should be evaluated using:

1. Control requirement
2. Available evidence
3. Identified weakness
4. Potential risk and business impact
5. Severity and priority
6. Recommended corrective action
7. Responsible owner
8. Target completion date

**Evidence principle:** An absence of evidence means the control is Not Verified. It does not automatically prove that the control is ineffective.

## 3. Gap Severity Classification

| Severity | Description | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Gap may expose individuals or the organization to severe harm | Immediate |
| High | Significant weakness affecting important AI governance controls | High |
| Medium | Moderate weakness requiring planned remediation | Medium |
| Low | Limited weakness with relatively low potential impact | Routine |

Severity should be based on the likelihood and impact of the identified risk, not merely the absence of documentation.

## 5. Priority Assessment

The following priorities are illustrative and must be validated through actual evidence and risk assessment.

| Priority | Gap IDs | Reason |

| Critical | GAP-03, GAP-05 | Risk assessment and fairness failures may lead to serious harm |
| High | GAP-01, GAP-04, GAP-06, GAP-07, GAP-08, GAP-09 | Weak accountability, privacy, oversight and operational safeguards |
| Medium | GAP-02, GAP-10 | Inventory and incident-process weaknesses require planned remediation |

## 6. Root Cause Analysis

Potential root causes to investigate include:

- Absence of formal AI governance policies.
- Unclear ownership of AI-related risks.
- Inadequate documentation and evidence retention.
- Limited technical testing and monitoring.
- Insufficient employee awareness and training.
- Weak third-party oversight.
- Lack of periodic independent assessments.

Root causes must be confirmed through interviews, documentation review and control testing.

## 7. Recommended Remediation Strategy

| Priority | Remediation Activity | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establish AI accountability and governance ownership | Clear responsibility |
| 2 | Conduct AI risk and impact assessments | Better risk identification |
| 3 | Perform fairness and data quality testing | Reduced discrimination risk |
| 4 | Strengthen privacy and security controls | Improved data protection |
| 5 | Establish human oversight procedures | More accountable decisions |
| 6 | Implement ongoing monitoring | Early detection of emerging risks |
| 7 | Establish incident reporting and escalation | Timely incident response |
| 8 | Conduct periodic compliance reviews | Continuous improvement |

## 8. Validation and Closure

A gap should only be closed after:

1. Corrective action has been implemented.
2. Supporting evidence has been collected.
3. The control has been tested.
4. The reviewer has assessed whether the weakness is adequately addressed.
5. Closure has been documented and approved.

A management statement or updated policy alone is not sufficient evidence of operating effectiveness.

## 9. Expected Outcomes

- Structured compliance gap register.
- Risk-based remediation priorities.
- Identified ownership and accountability.
- Evidence-based corrective action.
- Improved AI governance maturity.
- Input for the Compliance Action Plan.

## 10. Disclaimer

This is an educational portfolio assessment based on a hypothetical AI recruitment system. The gaps are illustrative scenarios, not verified organizational findings or a formal ISO/IEC 42001 certification assessment.
